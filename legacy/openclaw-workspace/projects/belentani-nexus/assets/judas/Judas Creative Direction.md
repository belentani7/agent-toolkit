# Judas — Creative Direction

## Core concept
A 239.184-second narrative music video for “Judas” by Belentani. Peter, played as the user's recognizable self, investigates and relives a seductive betrayal by Judas. The industrial blueprint and interface language is a diegetic metaphor for memory analysis, desire, shame, emotional damage, and the attempt to map a person who cannot be reduced to data. It must never read as a product advertisement or industrial showcase.

## Narrative spine
Peter begins in care and intimate attachment. Judas is alluring, physically present, and dangerous: a real male lover whose tenderness and deception coexist. Peter is seduced, notices contradictions and lies, and repeatedly returns to the kiss despite knowing the cost. Religious and forensic imagery externalizes the conflict: a holy crime, a broken crown, a stolen soul, shame, and the sword. The ending does not resolve into clean escape; Peter is called Peter and still chooses to kiss Judas again, making repetition the final emotional decision.

## Protagonist continuity lock
Peter is the central protagonist in every major chapter, not a silhouette or reference-only figure. Preserve the user's recognizable facial identity, facial proportions, long curly hair, beard, skin, costume language, and emotional performance. Use the supplied character portraits as identity anchors and the interaction images as performance/composition anchors. Performance progression: protective care → seduction and surrender → suspicion → shame and injury → lucid relapse.

## Judas continuity lock
Judas is a real alluring and dangerous adult male lover. Keep his presence human, tactile, intimate, and emotionally specific. His danger is expressed through gaze, proximity, touch, withheld truth, and betrayal—not through an object, machine, product, or abstract industrial entity.

## World and visual grammar
Cinematic live-action narrative with restrained symbolic compositing. Primary spaces: intimate bedroom, rain-soaked urban street, chapel/cathedral, and dark analytical interface space that remains diegetically connected to Peter's investigation. Use symmetrical tableaux, low-key portraiture, shallow depth of field, rain, candlelit ritual, reflective surfaces, suspended geometric lines, and controlled frontal/low-angle compositions. Blueprint/UI elements may label memory fragments and the search for “JUDAS,” but typography must be sparse, legible, and subordinate to the human drama.

## Lyrics-led chapter architecture
The production plan must derive exact time ranges and chapter boundaries from the master audio and the supplied full lyrics. Chapters must cover: care and the first intimate bond; seduction and repeated kisses; recognition of lies; holy crime and broken crown; stolen soul, shame, and sword imagery; the direct naming of Peter; and the final chosen return to Judas. Do not invent lyric text or alter lyric meaning.

## Technical locks
Master music source: `Judas demo pura de Pedro Belentani.mp3`, measured duration 239.184 seconds. Final delivery: 1280x720, 16:9, 60 fps. If generation is available only at 768P, post must deterministically convert to 1280x720 and conform to constant 60 fps while preserving sync and the full song duration. No subtitle burn-in unless explicitly requested later. Preserve the master song as the final music layer; generated/native clip audio is disposable sync support or scene ambience only and must not replace the master.

## Reference handling
New references in attachment order are the primary story, identity, and scene continuity set. Earlier references may supplement them but may not override identity or relationship continuity. Treat visible watermark/UI remnants in references as carrier artifacts, not required story elements.
