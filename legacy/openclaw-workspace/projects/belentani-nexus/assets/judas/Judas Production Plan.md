# Judas — Lyrics-Led Production Plan

## Logline
Peter, embodied by the recognizable user, cares for and falls for Judas, an alluring and dangerous human male lover. Even after Peter recognizes the lies, the holy crime, the broken crown, the stolen soul, the shame, and the sword, he repeatedly chooses the kiss—and finally chooses it again with full knowledge of the betrayal.

## Treatment
The video is a serious cinematic live-action narrative for the complete 239.184-second master song. It is not a dance video, performance video, fashion presentation, commercial, or choreography-led music video. Peter is never a background silhouette: his recognizable face, long curly hair, beard, facial proportions, skin, costume continuity, and emotional performance carry the story. Acting is restrained and human, with deliberate blocking, natural physical interaction, and story-driven pacing. Judas is always a real human lover whose seduction and danger are expressed through touch, gaze, proximity, withheld truth, and betrayal.

The visual language should suggest large-format / IMAX-style scale and composition without spectacle replacing character: monumental chapel and street spaces, strong depth, carefully controlled camera movement, intimate close-ups, grounded lens perspective, and deliberate transitions. Avoid dance beats, synchronized body movement, fashion posing, runway presentation, product beauty shots, and commercial presentation.

The visual world moves between a bedroom of care, rain-soaked urban streets, a sacred chapel/cathedral, and a dark diegetic analytical space where Peter tries to map memories and identify Judas. Blueprint/UI graphics are only a visual metaphor for memory analysis and emotional damage. They must remain sparse, subordinate to the human bodies, and never read as commercial product design.

## Lyrics-led chapters

### Chapter 1 — Care before the fall (0.000–45.000)
Use the intro and opening verse to show Peter drying Judas's hair, removing his jacket, laying him to rest, whispering for him to stay, then watching the lover's human presence with curiosity and desire. The bedroom reference establishes protective intimacy. Use quiet blocking and held close-ups rather than choreography. Transition from close care to Judas's thunderous entrance without changing the relationship into an object study.

### Chapter 2 — Seduction and warning (45.000–75.000)
Judas enters with allure and concealed danger. Rain, boots, chains, direct eye contact, and close physical distance express the lyric's storm and flame. Peter leans into Judas despite feeling cold. The urban street and face-to-face reference establish the lovers' equal physical reality before the power imbalance becomes explicit. Movement remains natural and motivated by the relationship, never dance-like.

### Chapter 3 — Holy crime and first relapse (75.000–98.000)
Peter recognizes Judas as a liar, then kisses him anyway. The first chorus should intercut human kisses, chapel/candle imagery, and restrained diegetic annotations that frame the memory as evidence rather than a technical product. “Holy crime” is a contradiction held in Peter's face and body. Camera movement is dramatic but grounded: slow pushes, controlled lateral movement, and deliberate reframing only when story or lyric emphasis requires it.

### Chapter 4 — Broken crown and stolen soul (98.000–128.000)
Judas remains beside Peter while the sky screams. The gold chain and broken crown become tactile relationship motifs. Judas writes his gospel across Peter's skin and bed through symbolic marks and gesture, then kisses Peter's head as Peter experiences the stolen-soul image. The interface may briefly register the memory, but Judas's living face and hands stay dominant. Use large-scale space for emotional isolation, not spectacle or posing.

### Chapter 5 — Required submission and betrayal image (128.000–151.000)
During the second “I kissed Judas” refrain, include the mandatory brief shot: the recognizable user/Peter kneels and kisses Judas's feet while Judas is visibly the other human lover. Stage it as an intentional emotional story moment, not an object insert, fashion image, or product shot. Judas's posture, gaze, and hand placement should convey alluring control; Peter's face and posture should convey submission, desire, devotion, humiliation, and betrayal at once. Connect the image to the lyric “I kissed Judas,” and return immediately to their human relationship rather than lingering on footwear or industrial detail. Keep the physical action simple, serious, and natural; no choreography.

### Chapter 6 — Peter named, shame and sword (151.000–171.000)
The bridge directly names Peter. Show him confronting his own reflection and the chapel witness, seeing the sword and feeling shame, while still orienting toward Judas's name. “I’m not done kissing him yet” is the transition from conscious self-knowledge into deliberate relapse. Use restrained acting, pauses, eye-lines, and controlled blocking.

### Chapter 7 — Repeated choice (171.000–194.000)
The third chorus repeats earlier gestures with changed emotional knowledge. Peter kisses Judas again, no longer as innocent surrender but as a lucid decision. Use the rain embrace, the sacred tableau, and close portraits as recurring visual anchors. The pacing follows the song's energy through cuts and camera pressure, never through dancing or choreographed performance.

### Chapter 8 — Memory cannot contain the lover (194.000–219.000)
In the breakdown, Peter challenges any system that thinks it knows his heart. The diegetic blueprint/UI fractures into memory layers, but the human bodies remain the source of truth. Judas pulls Peter in and teaches him to live with sin; Peter accepts the contradiction. Let the analytical space feel monumental and empty while the acting stays intimate and grounded.

### Chapter 9 — Final flame and unresolved return (219.000–239.184)
The final chorus turns the confrontation into mutual recognition: Judas knows them, Peter lights the match, Judas takes the flame, and Peter kisses him again. End on both men as living human subjects, with Peter still recognizable and the final “Peter is how you call me, man...” landing unresolved rather than redeemed or commercially triumphant. Use a controlled final hold and the song's natural landing rather than a performance climax.

## Emotional arc
- **Start:** Protective, tender, attentive Peter.
- **Turn:** Seduced by Judas while sensing the lie.
- **Peak:** Holy crime, broken crown, stolen soul, feet-kiss submission, shame, sword, and repeated return.
- **Final:** Lucid relapse; Peter chooses the kiss again.
